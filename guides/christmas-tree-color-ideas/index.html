What are the best Christmas tree color schemes?

Three formulas always work: brown and gold (brown velvet ribbon with champagne mercury glass), red and white (red and ivory ornaments with striped ribbon), and ribbon as the garland (camel velvet and champagne ribbon swags). Stick to two colors and finish with a woven tree collar.

A tree looks designer when it sticks to two colors and one texture. These are the three formulas trending most right now, each with its short shopping list. All three sit in the same woven tree collar.

Formula 1: Brown and gold

The most searched tree look this season. Warm, quiet and grown up.

  1. 4 inch brown velvet ribbon in loose vertical cascades from the top down.
  2. Champagne mercury glass ornaments tucked deep into the branches.
  3. Neutral velvet bows clipped in the gaps.

Formula 2: Red and white

  1. Red ball ornaments, 24 pack.
  2. Ivory ball ornaments mixed in evenly with the red.
  3. Red and white striped ribbon tucked in cascades.

Formula 3: Ribbon as the garland

  1. Camel velvet wired ribbon wrapped in loose horizontal swags.
  2. Champagne metallic wired ribbon layered between the velvet.
  3. A few champagne ornaments only. The ribbon is the star.

The rules for all three

  • Lights first, ribbon second, ornaments last.
  • Plan about 10 yards of ribbon for a 7.5 ft tree.
  • Finish with the woven tree collar so the base looks as good as the top.

Frequently asked questions

How do you decorate a brown and gold Christmas tree?

Use 4 inch brown velvet ribbon in vertical cascades, champagne mercury glass ornaments and neutral velvet bows.

How much ribbon do you need for a Christmas tree?

About 10 yards for a 7.5 foot tree.

What order do you decorate a Christmas tree?

Lights first, then ribbon, then ornaments, then the tree collar.
